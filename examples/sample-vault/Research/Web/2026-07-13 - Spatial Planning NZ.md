# Spatial Planning NZ

## For future Claude

Research note on spatial planning in New Zealand, compiled 2026-07-13 for [[people/Ali Kimber]] at [[Projects/Nelson City Council]]. Covers the legislative history (RMA to SPA/NBA to Planning Bill), the current reform trajectory under the National-led government, and Nelson City Council's own spatial plan (Te Ara o Whakatu). This is a fast-moving policy area -- verify legislation status before citing.

## Summary

New Zealand's planning system is undergoing its most significant reform in 30+ years. The Resource Management Act 1991 (RMA) is being replaced. The previous Labour government passed two replacement acts in August 2023, but the incoming National-led coalition repealed them in December 2023 and introduced its own Planning Bill in 2025.

## The repealed Spatial Planning Act 2023

- Passed 15 Aug 2023, royal assent 23 Aug 2023
- Required each region to develop a **regional spatial strategy** setting out long-term issues, opportunities, and challenges
- Strategies had to align with regional geographical boundaries
- Required regional planning committees to comply with Maori consultation arrangements
- Promoted integration across NBA, Land Transport Management Act 2003, Local Government Act 2002, and Water Services Entities Act 2022
- **Repealed 23 December 2023** by the incoming National-led government

## The current reform: Planning Bill 2025

The National government's replacement approach splits into two acts:

1. **Natural Environment Act** -- managing the natural environment
2. **Planning Act (Planning Bill 2025)** -- planning to enable development and infrastructure

### Key features of the Planning Bill

- **30-year regional spatial plans** identifying growth areas, infrastructure corridors, and areas needing protection
- Each region must have a **spatial plan** focused on:
  - Sufficient future urban development areas
  - Development areas prioritised for public investment
  - Existing and planned infrastructure corridors and strategic sites
- **Combined plans**: each plan includes a spatial planning chapter, an environment chapter, and planning chapters (one per territorial authority district)
- **Nationally standardised land-use zones** -- NZ currently has 1,175 different zone types; this will be significantly reduced
- Councils must work together to agree one combined plan per region
- Territorial authorities will jointly make and maintain a spatial plan with regional councils

## Nelson City Council: Te Ara o Whakatu

Nelson has its own **City Centre Spatial Plan** -- Te Ara o Whakatu (The Pathways of Nelson):

- **Adopted:** October 2021 by unanimous council vote
- **Scope:** 30-year vision for Nelson city centre
- **Public engagement:** 330+ submissions, 70% in favour, 12% opposed
- **Eight transformative actions** integrating culture, environment, streets, precincts, developments, places, and laneways

### Key goals

- Grow city centre population **tenfold** to at least 1,000 residents
- Solutions include adapting vacant buildings above shops and medium-density housing
- **City greening programme**: transform major streets into leafy boulevards with 1,000 new street trees (native and exotic)
- People-focused street design
- Short, medium, and long-term implementation phases

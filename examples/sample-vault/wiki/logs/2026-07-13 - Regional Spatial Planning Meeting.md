# Regional Spatial Planning Meeting

## For future Claude

Cross-council meeting held 2026-07-13 on regional spatial planning under the Planning Bill 2025. Key outcomes: select committee report due 2026-07-20, regional spatial plan must be developed within 15 months of enactment (including 20-day consultation), joint process agreement with [[people/Tasman District Council]] to be drafted by end of 2026, spatial planning committee to be established. Implementation expected around 2030. This is the foundational meeting for [[Projects/NCC Joint Spatial Planning]].

## Key facts

- **Select committee report due:** 2026-07-20 (as of 2026-07-13, meeting discussion)
- **Regional spatial plan timeline:** must be developed within **15 months of enactment**, including a 20-day consultation period
- **Expected implementation:** around 2030 (as of 2026-07-13, meeting discussion)
- **Draft national direction on spatial planning:** expected release August 2026
- **New system structure:** spatial plans + land use plans + natural environment plans
- **Spatial plan has statutory teeth** -- unlike the previous future development strategy, this is legally binding
- **Less discretion and community input** under the new system compared to current RMA process
- **Geographic scope:** broader than future development strategy -- covers rural and urban areas, growth areas, infrastructure corridors, climate change adaptation

## Legislative status

- Bill has passed first reading
- Select committee report due **2026-07-20**
- Final product still uncertain -- potential changes in the bill
- Timeline is tight, with potential amendments post-election
- National direction for spatial planning: draft expected August 2026, final national direction process TBD
- Election may impact legislative process -- potential changes to national direction and regulatory relief depending on coalition

## Key discussion points

### Statutory weight
The new spatial plan has statutory teeth compared to the previous future development strategy. It will guide future regulatory and land use plans with binding effect.

### Scope
Broader than FDS -- covers rural and urban areas, identifies growth areas, addresses infrastructure and climate change adaptation. Includes social infrastructure. Integration of climate adaptation planning with priority areas identified.

### Regulatory relief regime
- Potential impact on significant natural areas and landscapes
- Financial implications being assessed
- Relationship between regulatory relief and land use plans discussed
- Narrowing of local government decision-making raised as a concern

### Timing concerns
- Tight deadlines raised as a challenge
- Community consultation windows are compressed
- Need for joint agreement before legislation is enacted
- Consequences of not meeting timelines: may need a second-generation plan
- High-level management awareness of changes confirmed as ongoing

### Election risk
- Upcoming election may impact legislative process
- Left-leaning coalition's stance on regulatory relief may differ
- National direction could change depending on election outcome
- Potential amendments post-election
